# Keystones

This section collects the central findings, claims, and contributions that support the dissertation.

## Current keystone propositions

1. **Laser triggering is a probabilistic channel-control problem, not a single intensity threshold.** The relevant state is defined by measured laser parameters, $E/N$, geometry, atmosphere and electrode history. See [[II Areas/01_Research/Theory - Laser-Triggered Breakdown and Switching\|Theory - Laser-Triggered Breakdown and Switching]].
2. **Delay and jitter are only meaningful with a timing and uncertainty budget.** Failed shots are censored physical observations, and literature values are not comparable without marker definitions and bandwidth. See [[II Areas/01_Research/Diagnostics - Timing EMP and Radiation\|Diagnostics - Timing EMP and Radiation]].
3. **The minimum original contribution is the causal link from measured laser/channel state to breakdown probability, timing and waveform.** See [[I Projects/LTSG Core Research Package 2026-2028\|LTSG Core Research Package 2026-2028]].
4. **EMP and ionising radiation require matched controls and independent diagnostics.** They are valuable extensions but must not be inferred from pickup-prone single channels. See [[II Areas/01_Research/Research Extensions Roadmap\|Research Extensions Roadmap]].
5. **Application and economic claims depend on measured reliability, recovery and lifetime.** They remain conditional until a pulsed-power demonstrator supplies those inputs.
