# Kerr Self-Focusing Creates Extended Filamentary Channels

## Core atomic concept

When ultrashort-pulse peak power exceeds the Kerr self-focusing threshold, nonlinear propagation can create an extended filament through the dynamic balance of self-focusing, diffraction, plasma defocusing and higher-order effects. A filament is not a passive Gaussian focus and its deposited-energy profile must be measured.

## Mathematical formulation

The refractive index is approximated as

$$n=n_0+n_2I.$$

For a near-Gaussian beam, a common critical-power estimate is

$$
P_{cr}\approx\frac{3.77\lambda^2}{8\pi n_0n_2}.
$$

The controlling ratio $P/P_{cr}$ is more relevant to self-focusing onset than pulse energy alone. A minimal propagation model is a nonlinear Schrödinger/unidirectional pulse equation containing diffraction, group-velocity dispersion, Kerr response, ionisation loss and plasma defocusing.

## Interpretation safeguards

- “Intensity clamping” is regime- and diagnostic-dependent, not a universal constant.
- Multiple filamentation and moving-focus effects can make one integrated image misleading.
- Nanosecond operation should not inherit femtosecond-filament assumptions; avalanche, thermal blooming and conventional focusing may dominate.

## Experimental consequences

- Measure channel length, continuity and position rather than labelling all elongated luminosity a filament.
- Record $P/P_{cr}$, focusing geometry, chirp, pulse duration and atmosphere.
- Treat filament triggering as an extension unless the installed laser actually operates in the required regime.
