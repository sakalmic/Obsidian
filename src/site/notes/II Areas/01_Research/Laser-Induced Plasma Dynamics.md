# Laser-Induced Plasma Dynamics

## Overview
Laser-induced breakdown (LIB) is a nonlinear optical and plasma process in which focused laser radiation at intensities of $I > 10^{10}\text{ W/cm}^2$ generates seed electrons in a gaseous dielectric through multiphoton ionisation, followed by avalanche ionisation driven by inverse bremsstrahlung.

---

## Key stages of plasma evolution
1. **Optical breakdown and seed ionisation** ($t < 5\text{ ns}$): The laser focus produces an electron density of $n_e > 10^{18}\text{ cm}^{-3}$.
2. **Shock-wave expansion and hydrodynamic relaxation** ($10\text{ ns} - 1\ \mu\text{s}$): Plasma temperatures exceed $T_e > 20\,000\text{ K}$, acoustic and shock waves develop, and the gas density decreases along the channel axis.
3. **Conductive-channel formation in the electric field** ($1 - 100\ \mu\text{s}$): Under the applied high voltage, the decaying plasma develops into a low-impedance arc.
